\section{Worked synthesis: selection is a separate program}
\subsection{Prove the stable shift}
\textbf{Problem.} Show that subtracting the same finite constant $c$ from
every logit leaves softmax unchanged in real arithmetic.

\textbf{Solution.} Factor out $\exp(-c)$ from numerator and denominator:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\exp(z_i-c)}{\sum_j\exp(z_j-c)}
=\frac{\exp(-c)\exp(z_i)}{\exp(-c)\sum_j\exp(z_j)}
=\frac{\exp(z_i)}{\sum_j\exp(z_j)}.
\end{equation}
Choosing $c=\max_jz_j$ makes all exponent arguments nonpositive. This avoids
positive exponential overflow, though tiny probabilities can still underflow.
The proof does not permit NaN inputs or subtraction producing nonfinite values
without a stated numerical policy.

\subsection{A nucleus is a smallest prefix}
\textbf{Problem.} Sorted probabilities are $(.55,.25,.15,.05)$ and the
threshold is $p=.7$. Which entries survive and what is their renormalization?

\textbf{Solution.} One entry gives mass $.55<.7$; two give $.80\geq.7$.
Keep the first two, then divide by $.80$, obtaining $(.6875,.3125)$.
Keeping only entries whose individual probability exceeds $.7$ would keep
nothing: that is not nucleus sampling.

\subsection{Fix the draw, not just the seed}
\textbf{Problem.} For those retained probabilities and $u=.8$, which entry is
selected by a cumulative categorical sampler?

\textbf{Solution.} The first interval ends at $.6875$; $.8$ lies in the second.
A fixed draw isolates distribution and interval logic from the pseudorandom
generator. A seeded end-to-end test adds coverage of draw consumption, but it
cannot by itself localize an incorrect distribution.
