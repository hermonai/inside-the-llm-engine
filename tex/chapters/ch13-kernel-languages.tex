\chapter{How Kernels Are Written Now: CUDA, Triton, Tile DSLs and Graph Capture}\label{ch:kernel-languages}

\begin{ChapterIntent}
A decode step launches hundreds of small kernels, and at small batch the
time spent launching them can rival the time spent running them. This chapter
surveys how production kernels are written now --- CUDA C++ with CUTLASS and
CuTe, Triton, Python DSLs such as CuTe DSL and TileLang, and NVIDIA's
tile-level CUDA --- and what each abstraction hides and exposes. It then
measures launch overhead and shows how CUDA graphs, fusion and compilers such
as \texttt{torch.compile} recover it, and why engines capture graphs per batch
shape.
\end{ChapterIntent}
