### Source notes for Chapter 8 (llama.cpp `d006858`, read 2026-09-27)

`build_attn_mha` (`src/llama-graph.cpp`): with flash attention, one
`ggml_flash_attn_ext` node per layer, precision set to F32; without it,
`ggml_mul_mat(k, q)` with `ggml_mul_mat_set_prec(kq, GGML_PREC_F32)` (comment: the op
"tends to require high floating point range"), `ggml_soft_max_ext(kq, kq_mask,
kq_scale, …)`, then `ggml_mul_mat(v, kq)` on the transposed V cache.
`build_attn_inp_kq_mask` creates an F32 mask of n_kv × n_tokens, cast to F16 only for
flash attention; `llama_kv_cache::set_input_kq_mask` (`src/llama-kv-cache.cpp`) fills it
in a host buffer with 0 or −∞ from cell positions and sequences (SWA and ALiBi in the
same tensor). The Metal `kernel_soft_max` makes three loops over a row (maximum;
exponentials, sum and store; scale). `OP_FLASH_ATTN_EXT_NQPSG` = 8 queries per
threadgroup and `OP_FLASH_ATTN_EXT_NCPSG` = 64 (`ggml-metal-impl.h`); the vector kernel
is used when fewer than 20 queries (`ggml_metal_op_flash_attn_ext_use_vec`). With
`flash_attn_type` auto (the `common` default), `llama_context` reserves a graph and
keeps flash attention only if every layer's attention node runs on the device of that
layer's cache.

Pro and M2 Ultra); `ggml-metal.cpp` sets `n_cb` to 1 and exposes no thread-count hook;
`GGML_METAL_FUSION_DISABLE`, `GGML_METAL_CONCURRENCY_DISABLE`. `ggml-metal-device.m`: compute
encoders of `MTLDispatchTypeConcurrent`, barriers with `memoryBarrierWithScope:
MTLBarrierScopeBuffers`; no indirect command buffers anywhere in `ggml-metal`.
`ggml-metal-ops.cpp`: a node runs concurrently unless it writes a range an earlier node since
the last barrier read or wrote, or reads one it wrote; ADD chains fuse up to eight; NORM or
RMS_NORM fuses with a following MUL and ADD; decode flash attention with `nwg > 1` writes
partials, inserts a barrier and dispatches the reduce kernel. `ggml-metal.metal`: 109
`kernel void` functions, 560 `host_name` instantiations. `ggml/src/ggml-cuda`: 61 `.cu`
files plus 116 in `template-instances/`; `ggml-cuda.cu`: CUDA graphs disabled below Ampere,
for split buffers, and for MUL_MAT_ID that is not quantized or exceeds the MMVQ batch limit;
warm-up of two calls with unchanged node properties; `cudaGraphExecUpdate`, else

## Limits

Single-machine results on a desktop in use. The Chapter 1 lab's weights are
random; it measures the cost of streaming and multiplying them, not a model.
The Chapter 2 counts are exact properties of the files; its FLOPs and bytes
per token are derived from them, not timed, and its expert-union figures
assume uniform routing.
The Chapter 3 lab measures the CPU only; its "compute roof" is one kernel's
rate, and its bandwidth roof is what one summing kernel reaches, not the
memory's peak.
The Chapter 8 lab times one CPU thread running scalar loops; it says nothing about
GPU attention, for which the chapter relies on llama.cpp's measurements and the
published papers. Its 20-bytes-per-score traffic model assumes that the softmax
kernel's re-reads of a row hit in cache; that was not measured directly.
The Chapter 9 depth runs are two repetitions each, with the load rising during the runs.
The Chapter 9 lab's one-thread runs compute rather than wait on memory; its bandwidth
figures count the cache once.
The Chapter 10 lab quantizes one projection of one model, whose inputs at layer 0 are
embeddings; deeper layers' inputs differ, and the methods' ranking can too. Its kernels
are the lab's own; llama.cpp's CPU kernels are hand-vectorized and faster.
The Chapter 11 lab's formats are the lab's own encoders, written from the
specifications; hardware converters may use other recipes (the MX specification allows
them), and the errors are for one layer of one model trained in 16 bits.
The Chapter 12 lab measures layer 0 only, over 3,217 tokens of one text; deeper layers'
keys, and contexts of tens of thousands of tokens, are not covered.
The Chapter 13 CPU lab times a worker thread in the same process, a far nearer
device than a GPU; its absolute costs say nothing about GPUs, only the order of
the methods. The Metal runs measure one small kernel on one Apple GPU in a busy
desktop session; the GPU time of a single waited-for command buffer varied from
5 to 12 µs between runs. The H100 figures in the chapter are Hazy Research's,
not measured here.
