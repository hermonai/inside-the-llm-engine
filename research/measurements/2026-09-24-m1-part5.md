## Questions

1. How many bytes of key/value cache, and of recurrent state, does llama.cpp
   allocate per token and per sequence for four local models with different
   attention layouts? (Chapters 25, 27, 29)
2. How does decode speed fall with context length for two 3B models with
   different grouped-query ratios, and for an all-attention 8B model against a
   hybrid 9B model? (Chapters 25, 27)
3. How many hidden tokens does a small reasoning model spend on easy
   arithmetic, and what do thinking budgets do to its answers? (Chapter 29)
4. How does a model with sliding-window layers behave as its prompt grows,
   next to one without? (Chapter 28)
5. How many tokens does an image become, and what does it cost to prefill?
   (Chapter 30)
6. Can a hybrid model reuse a cached prefix, and when? (Chapter 27)
7. What does llama.cpp's expert matrix product cost as a batch's expert union
   grows? (Chapter 26)

## Results

### Cache and state allocations (`kvsizes`)

llama-server with one slot and a 16,384-token context, flash attention on,
F16 cache:

| Model | Layers with a KV cache | KV cache | Per token | Recurrent state |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Llama 3.2 3B (24 : 8 heads) | 28 | 1,792 MiB | 112 KiB | — |
| Qwen2.5-Coder 3B (16 : 2) | 36 | 576 MiB | 36 KiB | — |
| Qwen3-8B architecture (32 : 8) | 36 | 2,304 MiB | 144 KiB | — |
| Qwen3.5-9B architecture (16 : 4, head 256) | 8 | 512 MiB | 32 KiB | 50.25 MiB per sequence: R (conv, F32) 2.25 MiB, S (state, F32) 48 MiB, "1 cells, 32 layers" |

Every figure equals 2 × layers × KV heads × head width × 2 bytes × 16,384
(derived) to the byte. The hybrid's server log reports the fused Gated
DeltaNet kernels enabled for both decode and chunked prefill.

### Decode speed against context (`depth`, mean of 2) and prefill (`pp`, mean of 3)

| Model | tg64 at 0 | at 4,096 | at 16,384 | pp512 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Llama 3.2 3B | 24.24 (23.64, 24.84) | 19.35 (18.87, 19.83) | 10.47 (9.85, 11.08) | 269.96 |
| Qwen2.5-Coder 3B | 24.36 (23.98, 24.73) | 22.46 (22.91, 22.02) | 17.81 (17.68, 17.94) | 293.88 |
| Qwen3-8B architecture | 10.47 (10.41, 10.53) | 8.67 (8.46, 8.89) | 5.56 (5.60, 5.51) | 110.59 |
| Qwen3.5-9B architecture | 7.83 (7.97, 7.68) | 7.22 (7.14, 7.31) | 7.83 (7.64, 8.02) | 103.16 |

Tokens per second. `llama-bench -d` fills the context before timing the 64
generated tokens. The two runs of one configuration differed by up to 12%
(Llama 3.2 3B at 16,384 tokens: 9.85 and 11.08), so the hybrid's higher value
at 16,384 than at 4,096 is within the noise; its speed does not measurably
fall with context.

Derived, in the chapters: a bandwidth-only model, v(S) = v(0) · W / (W + S·k)
with W the tensor bytes and k the cache bytes per token, predicts 19.65 and
22.59 at 4,096 tokens and 12.53 and 18.54 at 16,384 for the two 3B models;
9.38 and 7.16 for the Qwen3-8B architecture.

### Reasoning with thinking budgets (`reasoning`)

DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B in `llama-server -np 1 -c 8192 --reasoning-format
deepseek`; chat completions at temperature 0 (seed 1), `max_tokens` 2,048,
`cache_prompt: false`, and a per-request `thinking_budget_tokens` of −1
(unlimited), 256 or 0, which the server turns into its reasoning-budget sampler
(`common/reasoning-budget.cpp`). Eight arithmetic problems written for this
probe (answers 145, 391, 105, 54, 9, 18, 2, 66), each ending with the
instruction to finish with a line "Answer: N". Token counts come from the
server's `/tokenize` applied to the returned `reasoning_content` and `content`.
Timings are the server's.

| Budget | Correct, requested format | Correct, value anywhere | Hit the 2,048 limit | Reasoning tokens (mean / median) | Answer tokens (mean) | Output tokens (mean) | Total output tokens | Seconds per answer (mean) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| unlimited | 6 / 8 | 7 / 8 | 1 | 1,237 / 1,201 | 166 | 1,407 | 11,255 | 144.3 |
| 256 | 7 / 8 | 8 / 8 | 0 | 256 / 256 | 377 | 637 | 5,096 | 61.7 |
| 0 | 7 / 8 | 7 / 8 | 1 | 0 / 0 | 1,148 | 1,152 | 9,213 | 109.2 |

"Value anywhere" also accepts the last `\boxed{N}` in the answer
(`reasoning_summary.py`). The runs that failed the strict check:

- unlimited, train trip (145 minutes): 2,047 reasoning tokens, limit reached
  while thinking, no answer;
- unlimited, 2¹⁰ mod 7: correct value given as `\boxed{2}`, no "Answer:" line;
- budget 256, rectangle area: correct value given as `\boxed{54}`, no
  "Answer:" line;
- budget 0, divisors of 36: 2,045 visible tokens, limit reached in the middle
  of listing candidate divisors, no answer.

With a budget of 0 the model wrote its reasoning in the visible answer: output
fell by 18% against the unlimited runs, where the 256-token budget cut it by
55%. Decode ran at 9.43–10.76 tokens per second across the 24 runs. A document
build (`make textbook`, 80 s) overlapped the unlimited-budget runs from
09:55:22 to 09:56:42 UTC; token counts and answers are unaffected, and no
timing comparison in the chapters depends on those runs.

### Image tokens (`vision`)

Gemma 4 E4B in Ollama (`num_ctx` 8192, `num_predict` 32, temperature 0),
prompt "Describe this image in one sentence." with no image, then with page 20
of the book's PDF rendered by `pdftoppm -scale-to` at 256, 768 and 1,536 pixels
on the long side, each sent twice.

| Image | Pixels | PNG bytes | Prompt tokens | Prefill, first | Prefill, repeat |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| none | — | — | 23 | 0.31 s | — |
| 256 | 182 × 256 | 19,402 | 105 | 1.32 s | 0.16 s |
| 768 | 544 × 768 | 91,946 | 201 | 1.89 s | 0.16 s |
| 1,536 | 1,087 × 1,536 | 248,947 | 761 | 7.89 s | 0.06 s |

Image tokens (prompt tokens minus the 23 of the text-only prompt): 82, 178 and
738. At 48 × 48 pixels per token (patch 16, 3 × 3 pooling; GGUF metadata),
the two larger images' areas predict 181 and 725 (derived); the smallest
predicts 20 and cost 82. Repeated requests reused Ollama's cached prompt,
image included. Decode ran at 15.2–18.2 tokens per second. The `response`
field was empty in every run: Gemma 4's thinking is on by default in Ollama,
and all 32 generated tokens went to it; the measurement uses only prompt
counts and timings.

### Prefix reuse with recurrent state (`hybrid_prefix`, `hybrid_prefix_b512`)

One `llama-server` at a time (`-np 1 -c 16384 -fa on`), five `/completion`
requests in order with `cache_prompt: true`, `n_predict` 8, temperature 0. The
document is the first 2,400 words of `doc.txt`; q1 and q2 are two one-line
questions; the edit inserts a short sentence at one third of the document's
characters (about token 1,570).

| Request | Llama 3.2 3B processed | Hybrid, defaults | Hybrid, checkpoint every 512 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| P1 document + q1 (cold) | 5,309 of 5,309 (21.5 s) | 5,343 of 5,343 (52.7 s) | 5,343 (59.3 s) |
| P2 document + q2 | 15 (0.21 s) | 516 (5.4 s) | 516 (5.5 s) |
| P3 document + q1 again | 15 (0.22 s) | 516 (5.4 s) | 516 (6.0 s) |
| P4 edited document + q1 | 3,750 (16.1 s) | 5,350 of 5,350 (54.5 s) | 5,350 (60.4 s) |
| P5 document + q1 after the edit | 1 (0.06 s) | 4 (0.35 s) | 4 (0.33 s) |

"Processed" is the server's `timings.prompt_n`. The hybrid's server log
(`hybrid_prefix-hybrid-*.server.log`) shows each step:

- defaults: two checkpoints per prompt, at 4,827 and 5,339 tokens (516 and 4
  before the end), 50.251 MiB each; P2 and P3 restored the one at 4,827; P4
  found none before position 1,573 and logged "forcing full prompt
  re-processing"; the saved prompt state was 318.0 MiB (Llama's: 581.5 MiB);
- `--checkpoint-every-n-tokens 512`: with the default logical batch of 2,048,
  checkpoints were made only at batch boundaries, at 2,048 and 4,096 tokens,
  plus the two near the end; P4 again found none before 1,573; saved state
  418.5 MiB;
- P5 in every configuration restored the unedited prompt from the host-memory
  prompt cache (`--cache-ram`), checkpoints included.

With `--checkpoint-every-n-tokens 512 -b 512` (`hybrid_prefix_b512`),
checkpoints fell every 512 tokens: eleven per prompt (512, 1,024, …, 4,608,
then 4,831 and 5,339), 553 MiB, and a saved prompt state of 770.3 MiB. P1
processed 5,343 tokens (53.1 s); P2 and P3 512 each (5.1 and 5.2 s),
restoring the checkpoint at 4,831; P4 restored the one at 1,536 and processed
3,814 tokens (37.5 s), against Llama's 3,750; P5 processed 4 (0.26 s).

The hybrid's answers began with a `<think>` block (the fine-tune's chat
behaviour); only processing counts and times are used.

### The expert matrix product (`moe`, `moesql`)

`test-backend-ops` built from the llama.cpp tree at `d006858` (ggml 0.9.11,
Release, Metal), `perf` mode, `MUL_MAT_ID` with 128 matrices of 2,048 × 768
(`k` = 2,048, `m` = 768; one of Qwen3-30B-A3B's expert projections), 8 used per
token, the token count `n` from 1 to 512, F32 activations, expert weights in
Q4_K or F16. The test draws distinct experts per token uniformly at random
(`init_mul_mat_id_tensors`). Each run reports the mean time per call over
many repetitions of the same call; three runs each.

timing columns; it is kept, and the runs were repeated with `--output sql`

Derived (`moe_summary.py`): an expert matrix is 884,736 bytes in Q4_K and
3,145,728 in F16. Below 32 tokens Metal runs a matrix–vector kernel per
selected expert (`ggml-metal-ops.cpp`), so each call reads 8n expert matrices:
61–69 GB/s for Q4_K and 62–63 GB/s for F16. From 32 tokens it groups tokens by
expert; reading the expected union once implies about 50 GB/s for F16 and
about 16 GB/s for Q4_K. The single-token figure (69.0 GB/s, at the M1's
nominal 68 GB/s) may be helped by on-chip caches, since perf mode repeats the
call over the same 7 MB of weights.

## Limits

- One machine under load from other work, as in Part IV. Absolute speeds are
  lower than on a quiet machine; comparisons within an experiment ran
  back to back.
- The Qwen3.5-9B file is a fine-tune, not Qwen's release; only its
  architecture is used.
- Gemma 4 ran in Ollama's engine, not llama.cpp, so its numbers are not
  directly comparable with the llama.cpp ones; the comparison in `ollama_ctx`
  is between two models in the same engine.
- No mixture-of-experts model was on the machine and none was downloaded for
  this record; the MoE measurement is of one layer's kernel with synthetic
  weights and uniformly random routing.
